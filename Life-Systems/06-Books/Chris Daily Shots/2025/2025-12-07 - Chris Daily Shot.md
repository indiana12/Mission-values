# 2025-12-07 Chris Daily Shot

- **Category**: Strategy / Geopolitics
- **Book**: *The 33 Strategies of War* — Robert Greene
- **Chapter / pages**: Strategy 6 “Segment Your Forces”
- **Source**: Kindle highlights (Loc. 865–930)

## Author’s Core Idea
Greene warns against fighting every battle on every front. Segment your forces—people, time, and attention—so critical campaigns get elite focus while lower-priority tasks run on autopilot. Concentration, not diffusion, wins wars.

## Mindmap Snapshot
- **Central node**: Segment and concentrate resources on decisive fronts.
- **Supporting node 1 →** Create strike teams for high-stakes missions; keep others in holding pattern.
- **Supporting node 2 →** Use decoys/automation to keep minor fronts quiet without draining leadership.
- **Guardrail node**: Spreading yourself thin invites defeat; being everywhere = being nowhere.

## Visualization Layer (author’s perspective)
- **Scene**: A general’s map with colored pins; only a few lines surge forward while others sit in reserve trenches.
- **Emotion/tone**: Calculated, cold focus—resist emotional pressure to fight every skirmish.

## Real-Life Application (general scenario)
A founder tries to grow five product lines simultaneously; marketing, ops, and finance scramble. Instead, they spin up a “strike team” for the highest-potential line, automate the others with basic support, and revisit them once the main front is secure.

## Implementation Nudge (today)
List current “fronts” (projects/commitments). Tag two as strike missions; push the rest into holding patterns (standard SOPs or pause). Inform stakeholders which efforts get elite focus this week.
